Six tracks, one country.

Rig King is set entirely in Australia, and each track is planted with the trees, shrubs and grasses of the place that inspired it. The tracks are fictional roads; the country is real. Together they form the difficulty ladder, from the home speedway to the Kimberley at night.

01 · Outback South Australia

Dusty Creek Speedway

Starting track · Free

A country dirt oval on the edge of a small outback town: red dirt, a covered grandstand like the old street-circuit stands, a concrete wall with a chain-link catch fence, sheds and a water tank out the back. This is where you learn to slide a car up two narrow ramps onto a moving trailer, with the whole town watching. The catch fence bounces you back onto the track, so mistakes cost time rather than the heat.

What's unique: the simplest rig route in the game and the one you will know by heart. No tunnels, no ferries, just the rig, the corners and everyone else wanting your spot.

Plants: river red gums along the creek and as the big shade trees by the grandstand, coolibah on the flats, western myall and belah across the plains, mulga, saltbush and bluebush, spinifex and blue flax lily tussocks between the gums. The grandstand garden has grevillea and kangaroo paws.

02 · Northern Rivers, New South Wales

Nightcap Drive

Rainforest road · Track pack

Inspired by the subtropical hinterland of northern New South Wales: green hills, dairy paddocks, a village of weatherboard houses, and a road that climbs through rainforest to a ridge tunnel. A showpiece waterfall falls into a lake alive with birds before the river runs down to the sea. The road is a closed street circuit, so it has guardrails, driveways and a tunnel portal that tall vehicles clip.

What's unique: elevation and compression. The tunnel entrance squeezes the pack together, the rig pushes cars toward the walls, and the van and bus scrape the lintel. Holding the deck through the climb and the bends is hard on purpose.

Plants: a canopy of yellow carabeen, flame trees and lilly pilly with strangler figs where their crowns clear the road; bangalow and cabbage tree palms; tree ferns, king ferns and elkhorns at the falls; an understorey of lomandra, cunjevoi, bird's nest fern, palm lily, native ginger and walking stick palm; plantation bananas on the lower slopes; gums on the higher ridge.

03 · Illawarra coast, New South Wales

Sea Cliff Run

Coast road and bridge · Track pack

Inspired by the Sea Cliff Bridge south of Sydney, where the road leaves the land and sweeps out over the ocean under sandstone cliffs. The run crosses the bridge at sunrise with hard barriers both sides and no shoulder, then dives into a huge illuminated cathedral tunnel in the headland, with a rockfall waiting at the exit. The tunnel is a game design choice, not a real place.

What's unique: the bridge. It is the most exposed boarding in the game, and side-by-side fighting over the water is a gamble. The AI holds its rockets for the tunnel, so expect fireworks in the dark.

Plants: no gums on this coast. Coastal rosemary heath over the slopes, low gnarled coastal banksias with pale flower spikes, coastal wattle, lomandra and pigface on the verges, and a stand of Norfolk Island pines on the headland. A mob of wallabies grazes a crest on the land side.

04 · The Daintree, Far North Queensland

Croc River Run

Point to point · Ferry · Trail · Track pack

Set in the Daintree, where a cable ferry crosses a crocodile river and a famous 4WD track runs north through the rainforest. The rig drives down the riverside road past the croc warning signs, boards the Mudlark Ferry under its own power, and the fight carries on aboard while the river slides past. On the far bank the road gives up: a bumpy, kerb-free trail through dense jungle, a splash creek, and a paddock-fence finish.

What's unique: it is the first point-to-point event and the only one with a parked trailer. On the ferry there is nowhere safe to go, and on the trail the bumps make the trailer itself hop. The cars run lifted suspension for this event only. Crocodiles watch from the banks; nothing in the game can hit them.

Plants: weeping paperbark, Leichhardt trees and lilly pilly along the river, forest red gums in the open forest, and grey and red mangroves on the mud at both banks. On the trail, Licuala fan palms and Alexandra palms, strangler figs, umbrella trees, Hope's cycads, a buttressed rainforest giant, king ferns and tree ferns, lawyer cane, elkhorns and bird's nest ferns on the trunks, hanging vines, cottonwood hibiscus on the creek banks, and a big-leaf edge of cunjevoi and native ginger. Bananas near the farms.

05 · Queensland to Western Australia

Long Haul Bash

Road train · Outback · Track pack

The long drive west. It starts as an ordinary motorway fight, narrows into farm country with cattle yards and kangaroo signs, and pulls into Long Ways Roadhouse, where the rig couples a second trailer and becomes a road train. Then the seal runs out: corrugations, whoops and red dirt all the way to the horizon, with emus crossing where they please. This is the road that leads to Sunset Gorge.

What's unique: the road train. You board the rear trailer, fight across its deck and cross two narrow steel tracks to the front trailer, and only the front trailer scores. The roadhouse is also the only place in the game to re-arm, once, if you are willing to leave the fight to do it.

Plants: gums and dry grass on the motorway verges; saltbush and bluebush, mulga and spinifex from the farm country onwards; ghost gums on the red dirt; cathedral termite mounds; dead trees, stumps and fallen logs; and wildflower clumps of Sturt's desert pea, mulla mulla and paper daisies along the farm acts. Old Canary Island date palms stand in the farmhouse gardens. Kangaroos, cattle and emus share the road.

06 · The Kimberley, Western Australia

Sunset Gorge

Red gravel · Sunset into night · Track pack

Inspired by the Kimberley after rain: red gravel, creek crossings with floodway markers, a billabong gone green, boabs, and the striped domes of the Bungle Bungles standing beside the road. The domes are scenery only, out of respect: no car, rig or rocket ever touches them, and rockets fizzle before they reach them. The heat starts at golden hour and ends under the stars, with headlights and marker lamps coming on along the way.

What's unique: the road is the opponent. Corrugations, washouts, hidden bumps and a signature trailer-hop keep the deck moving under you, and the gorge section runs between rock walls with water streaks down their faces. The dark changes how you read the track, but never how the cars drive.

Plants: boabs by the road and across the infield, Kimberley fan palms on the gorge floor and around the billabong, snappy gums, spinifex and golden tussock, saltbush and bluebush in the dry country, pandanus at the water with reeds, water lilies, crinum and blue flax lilies on the banks, and cathedral termite mounds. Egrets and ducks work the billabong; an emu mob crosses the road.

Training road

Your first drive is a sealed country road into Dusty Creek: paddock fences, gums, a shed, a water tank and a windmill, with the speedway signs counting down. Try the rocket, the oil and the tyre on the way, then catch the truck and get aboard.
